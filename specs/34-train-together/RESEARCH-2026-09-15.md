# Train Together — product research and validation

15 September 2026 · Discussion evidence; no implementation or recruitment authorized. Complements requirements/design/tasks; recommendations below are hypotheses, not signed-off requirements.

## Recommendation

Position this around **less phone admin when training together, with accurate progress for each person**. Live synchronization enables that benefit; it is not the benefit itself. Friends, nearby discovery and reusable plans can remain one coordinated release, with separate validation for each. An existing pair is the strongest initial test because they already have a reason to train together; nearby strangers introduce a separate matching problem.

## What empirical evidence supports

A randomized trial assigned 790 university participants to social comparison/support conditions during an 11-week exercise-class programme. Adjusted analysis associated the comparison intervention with 62% more attendance (95% CI 5–119%); social support had no significant effect. Networks were artificially assigned, rewards were offered, and the sample came from one university. This supports testing specific social mechanisms, not assuming all social features help. It does **not** predict Persistence retention, paid conversion, strength improvement, or the value of synchronizing two friends’ sets. Avoid importing its uplift into forecasts. [Zhang et al., 2016, primary paper](https://ndg.asc.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Support-or-Competition-pdf.pdf)

## Risks to resolve in the specification

- **Two paid memberships:** preserve the stated Premium/Premium Plus/eligible-coach requirement. Measure invitation attempts blocked by eligibility separately from eligible join failures. Interview mixed paid/free pairs as well as subscribers; an all-paid usability cohort cannot establish acquisition potential. Do not silently introduce guest access.
- **Empty nearby results:** useful discovery needs compatible people at a compatible venue and time, not simply accounts in a radius. Test one or two populated venues, show honest empty states and allow direct invitations. Never fabricate activity. Private-by-default visibility, host approval and block/report belong in discovery acceptance criteria.
- **Different workouts and bodies:** require independent loads, reps, rest and finish times. Test one athlete substituting an exercise or skipping it without changing the partner’s records. Injury details need not be collected or disclosed to make substitutions work.
- **More admin than before:** partner logging might double one person’s work. Observe actual phone interactions and corrections; compare logging-own-sets with explicit permission to log for a partner.
- **Loss of trust:** a stale screen must not appear synchronized. Test network loss, app termination, duplicate submissions and conflicting edits. Preserve acknowledged sets and exactly one history per athlete. Recovery must not depend on the partner staying connected.
- **Competition changes behaviour:** first test whether pairs want cooperation, comparison or both. Total kilograms disadvantages lighter/newer athletes; do not equate it with fair competition. Scoring needs separate acceptance criteria before inclusion.

## Validation before and after implementation approval

**Now: behavioural discovery.** Recruit only after Brad agrees to the research activity. Interview 8 existing training pairs: four both eligible, four mixed eligibility where feasible. Include differing experience and mixed iOS/Android use. Observe their existing logging method during a normal session with consent. Test a clearly labelled interactive prototype: invite, join, log different loads, substitute an exercise, lose connection, finish separately. Use an explicitly simulated eligible-account scenario for the join-usability task; separately test the real paid-eligibility explanation with mixed pairs. Prototype access does not grant product access or demonstrate willingness to pay. A prototype can test understanding; it cannot prove synchronization reliability.

**After approved implementation and Brad’s build:** pilot with 8–12 eligible pairs for three weeks, aiming for at least three natural shared sessions per pair. Include weak gym connectivity. Record the same pairs’ baseline logging interactions first; avoid changing their training programme for the experiment. Compensate research time, if agreed, rather than rewarding continued use. Show raw counts alongside percentages. This cohort finds usability failures and repeat-use signals, not statistically reliable retention or revenue uplift.

### Proposed decision criteria — not industry benchmarks

| Question                 | Proposed evidence threshold                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Can pairs start unaided? | At least 7 of 8 prototype pairs invite/join and identify whose records change, without moderator help.                                                                                                                                                |
| Does logging improve?    | At least 6 of 8 observed pairs show fewer total logging interactions than baseline, without increased record errors. Record actual time too.                                                                                                          |
| Do they choose it again? | At least 6 of the first 10 activated pilot pairs complete a second shared session within 14 days without a researcher reminder. Report cancellations and missing opportunities separately.                                                            |
| Is it trustworthy?       | Zero lost acknowledged sets, cross-athlete results, or duplicate histories in scripted recovery scenarios and the pilot; every failure blocks release until resolved. Existing stable-network p95 ≤2-second propagation target remains a test target. |
| Can nearby work?         | Log real searches, zero-result rate, requests, acceptance and completed sessions at pilot venues; inspect every unmatched request before expanding. No population-level matching claim from a tiny cohort.                                            |

Primary funnel: invite attempted → recipient opens → eligibility passes → join approved → both record → both histories saved → pair repeats. Use pair/session identifiers with minimal analytics payloads, not exercise or health details. Upgrade interest and actual purchases are separate outcomes; interview enthusiasm is not revenue proof.

## Interview prompts

1. Walk me through the last workout you did with someone. Who chose it and where did each of you record it?
2. Show an example of what you copied or changed between you.
3. When did your exercises, weights or finish times differ? What happened?
4. When did either phone last interrupt the session or lose signal?
5. How did you arrange your last session with someone new? What information mattered?
6. Which training tools do you each currently pay for? What made you start or stop?
7. After the prototype: explain what happens to your friend’s record when you change this set.

## Naming shortlist

**Persistence Together** is the preferred working name: clear and broad enough for friends and discovery. Alternatives: **Train Together**, **Training Partners**, **Shared Sessions**. Use “Train together” as the action label regardless. These are naming ideas only; no trademark, domain or store-name clearance has been performed.
